# 3-minute demo script

Record the phone screen with audio. Keep every claim true to what is on screen. Say "headphones" early.
Film with the hero picture pre-analyzed once (backend cache warm), but do one honest fresh analyze on camera.

**0:00 to 0:15, disclosure (required first line)**
"Resonant is an app I started before the hackathon. I brought over its name, logo, look and gesture design. Everything you will see about exploring pictures, from the Meta models to the touch and sound, was built during the hackathon."

**0:15 to 0:35, the problem**
Show a textbook diagram. "Screen readers can read the caption. They cannot tell a blind student where the sun is, how big the mountain is, or what sits next to what. Resonant makes the picture itself touchable. Headphones on — left and right matter."

**0:35 to 1:00, instant samples (screen covered or eyes closed)**
Home shows the sample gallery. "Two pictures work with no server and no key." Open the rainfall chart: let the audience hear the summary — "5 bars. Tallest: April bar, 80 millimetres." Step the left edge once: "Bar 5 of 5, about 40 percent of the tallest." Back out, open the water cycle, drag once across the sun so they hear a name, a buzz and a tone.

**1:00 to 2:00, a real picture**
Choose the hero picture. While it loads, say what is happening and let the progress speak: "Muse Spark names the parts. SAM 3.1 draws the exact outline of each — you can hear it counting them off." Drag across it: each part speaks, the tone rises and falls with height and swings left and right. Rest on empty space: it says which way the nearest part is. Quick tap: the description, and what is around it. Three-finger hold: the map in words. Then hold and ask "Where is the sun?" — drag, rest, hear the directions, land on it.

**2:00 to 2:15, what the finger feels (eyes open, for sighted viewers)**
Tap "Show outlines". "Every white outline is a mask SAM drew — this is what the finger has been feeling." Toggle it back off.

**2:15 to 2:45, lesson, quiz, tutor**
Tap **Lesson**: swipe the left edge through two spoken steps while each part outlines itself. Tap **Quiz me**: "Find the sun" — touch the wrong part first (low buzz, "That is the cloud. Try again."), then the right one (rising chime). Answer one question out loud. Finish: "Practise missed" brings the missed ones back. Then hold and ask a question, and a follow-up — "and why?" — to show the tutor remembers. Say: "Lessons, quizzes and a voice tutor from my earlier app, rebuilt on top of the picture — and the map, the guide, the ranks and the notes are computed on the phone, from the masks."

**2:45 to 3:00, how Meta's models are used**
Show the pipeline from the README. "Muse Spark decides what the parts are, writes the lesson and the quiz, and tutors. SAM 3.1 returns pixel-accurate masks. The mask is what lets a finger land on a river or a lung, not a rectangle — and it is what grades the quiz."

**3:00 to 3:10, close**
"Open source, public repo, link below. It runs on an iPhone from a link, and the recents shelf keeps the session's pictures one tap away. Next: proving live voice on the phone, and a library that lasts between sessions."
