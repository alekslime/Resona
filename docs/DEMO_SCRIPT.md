# 3-minute demo script

Record the phone screen with audio. Keep every claim true to what is on screen. Say "headphones" early.

**0:00 to 0:15, disclosure (required first line)**
"Resonant is an app I started before the hackathon. I brought over its name, logo, look and gesture design. Everything you will see about exploring pictures, from the Meta models to the touch and sound, was built during the hackathon."

**0:15 to 0:40, the problem**
Show a textbook diagram. "Screen readers can read the caption. They cannot tell a blind student where the sun is, how big the mountain is, or what sits next to what. Resonant makes the picture itself touchable."

**0:40 to 1:50, the demo (screen covered or eyes closed)**
Choose a real picture. While it loads, say what is happening: "Muse Spark names the parts. SAM 3.1 draws the exact outline of each." Drag across it: let the audience hear each part speak, the tone rise and fall with height and swing left and right. Rest on empty space: it says which way the nearest part is. Quick tap: the description, and what is around it. Three-finger hold: the map in words. Then hold and ask "Where is the sun?" — drag, rest, hear the directions, land on it.

**1:50 to 2:20, ask and edge gestures**
Hold a finger still, ask a question about the part. Swipe the left edge to step through the parts. Drag the right edge to change speech speed. "The same gestures as my earlier app, now with a picture under the finger."

**2:20 to 2:50, lesson, quiz, tutor (the Resonant-AI port)**
Tap **Lesson**: swipe the left edge through two spoken steps while each part outlines itself. Tap **Quiz me**: "Find the sun" — touch the wrong part first (low buzz, "That is the cloud. Try again."), then the right one (rising chime). Answer one question out loud. Finish: "Practise missed" brings the missed ones back. Then hold and ask a question, and a follow-up — "and why?" — to show the tutor remembers. Say: "Lessons, quizzes and a voice tutor from my earlier app, rebuilt on top of the picture — and the map, the guide and the notes are computed on the phone, from the masks."

**2:50 to 3:05, how Meta's models are used**
Show the pipeline from the README. "Muse Spark decides what the parts are, writes the lesson and the quiz, and tutors. SAM 3.1 returns pixel-accurate masks. The mask is what lets a finger land on a river or a lung, not a rectangle — and it is what grades the quiz."

**3:05 to 3:15, close**
"Open source, public repo, link below, and it runs on an iPhone from a link. Next: charts that read their values as you slide along them."
